Ada Lovelace and Bridgerton have a lot in common.<br>

During the early 19th Century the United Kingdom lived through four monarchs in twenty years. The latter of which – Queen Victoria – reigned for a total of 63 years, when she died in 1901 (Maurer, 2007). Only Queen Elizabeth the 2nd was able to break that record in 2015 and she outlasted her Great-Grandmother by 7 Years of Reign. During the Victorian Age the Industrial Revolution hit with full force, which caused an economical boom. Within a few years the population grew rapidly, which led to new industrial cities developing which also served as a trade hotspot (McCord, 2007). These developments led to new technical inventions and innovations. The first trains for coal transportation started operating in 1821, only a few years later passenger trains were allowed on the ever-growing railway system. Telegraphs got connected to the railways and the Penny Post followed shortly after – the postage stamp (casually) getting invented along the way (Maurer, 2007). Not only the economic changes show the Zeitgeist of the first half of the 19th Century, but also political changes were pursued. Amongst other things, the Chartists tried to implement a secret and universal suffrage, however that was only ment for men (☹) (Maurer, 2007). With all those pursuits for a democratical life, society was deeply divided into upper, middle and lower class. The upper class was often represented by the aristocrats, which governed the political system and regulated social life, by appointing ministers and marrying exclusively in their circles to keep others out (They were all related by the end of it). However – a change in these strict norms was still inevitable, the age of marriage rose, especially in the labour/ middle/ working class (ask the council)(McCord,2007). In this age of persistent and rapid change, with high prospects towards women and men, Ada Lovelace managed to question those norms with her mathematical gift – whether knowingly or unknowingly. <br>
